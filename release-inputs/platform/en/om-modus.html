ABOUT MODUS

From my own music studies
to a tool for others.

My name is Simen Raaen and I created MODUS. This website grew out of my own music studies and a wish to connect theory more closely with what I play and hear.

An independent music project by Simen Raaen.

BEHIND THE PROJECTSimen Raaen

Guitar. Music theory.
Composition and improvisation.

Read the story ↓

It began with my own learning.

As I study music theory, improvisation and composition, the same questions keep coming up: Why does this chord connection work? What gives a mode its character? How can what I understand on paper become something I actually hear and use?

MODUS grew as a way to explore these questions. I wanted to bring explanations, notes and the instrument closer together, shortening the path from a theoretical idea to practical playing.

Understand the connection. Hear the difference.

A scale becomes more interesting when it meets a chord. A progression becomes clearer when you can follow the notes inside it. And an explanation takes on new value when you can try it straight away.

That is why MODUS combines backing tracks, theory articles, audio examples and maps for guitar and piano. You can read about an idea, listen to it and take it to your instrument without starting over each time.

One map, more musical choices.

An important starting point for me is seeing the connections in music. You do not necessarily need a new scale map every time the chord changes. Often you can keep the same notes and explore which ones take on new roles against the harmony.

This does not mean every note works the same way over every chord. It means the map can stay clear while your listening and musical choices become more precise.

A project that grows with my studies.

MODUS is not a final answer to how music should be learned. It is a project that grows through my own studies, questions and practical experiments. The aim is to make music theory more accessible and useful without losing its nuances.

The nature-inspired appearance is a deliberate choice too. For me, nature and music are connected as sources of attention and inspiration. The visuals should leave room to focus on the sound, the ideas and the playing.
